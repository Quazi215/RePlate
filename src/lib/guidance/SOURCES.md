# Shosai's reviewed guidance context

Basics reviewed October 10, 2026 for slice #19; work-rule coverage added and reviewed October 11, 2026. `sources.server.ts` contains short paraphrases of the evidence below, not just a list of URLs. Requests do not retrieve websites. Recheck these pages before the demo and update both context and review dates if facts change.

- [NYC SNAP guide](https://www.nyc.gov/main/services/snap-benefits): SNAP basics; application, interview, and requested-document steps; HRA determination; alternative application methods; screening caveat and right to apply. Numeric benefit statements, expedited timelines, and recertification specifics are deliberately excluded.
- [NYC HRA ABAWD update](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hra/help/able-bodied-adults-without-dependents.page): October 1, 2026 compliance-calendar reset and reapplication; requesting a case change when the household still receives SNAP; November 1, 2025 implementation versus March 1, 2026 compliance. The reset does not remove work requirements or guarantee approval or automatic restoration.
- [NYC monthly work-rule guide](https://www.nyc.gov/main/services/snap-benefits/abawd): ABAWD terminology, ages 18–64 and child-under-14 boundary, documented exemptions, 80-hour work option and approved alternatives, three-month/36-month time limit, reporting, ACCESS HRA website status checks, and PACE help. Volunteer hours differ from job hours; exact calculations, earnings alternatives, and detailed restoration procedures are omitted. Do not treat ABAWD exemptions as exemptions from every SNAP rule or predict a person's status from age alone.
- [USDA October 2025 implementation memorandum](https://fns-prod.azureedge.us/snap/obbb-abawd-exemptions-implementation): July 4, 2025 enactment versus October 3 guidance, expanded ages and changed child boundary, removed status-based exceptions, new statutory Indian exceptions, and the distinction between general work requirements and ABAWD rules for ages 60–64. The official USDA PDF returned 403 during review; the memorandum text was reviewed in the [Arkansas legislature's official meeting packet](https://www.arkleg.state.ar.us/Home/FTPDocument?path=%2FAssembly%2FMeeting+Attachments%2F040%2F27884%2FE.9.c+DHS+DCO+SNAP+TEA+and+Work+Pays+Prgm+Updates+and+Relevant+Acts.pdf), PDF pages 81–86. The source link resolves to USDA's accessible publication page. Local timing and October 2026 reset come from HRA, not this federal memo.
- [New York State application guide](https://www.ny.gov/services/apply-snap): gross income before taxes/deductions; different income guidelines; official application and budget requirement. No numeric income limits or benefit amounts enter the guidance context.
- [7 CFR 273.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-C/part-273/subpart-A/section-273.1): general household definition and required combinations, including spouses, children under 22 living with parents, and children under another member's parental control. The eCFR view was current through October 8, 2026 when reviewed. Exceptions exist; Shosai refers unclear individual arrangements to HRA. USDA's public eligibility page could not be fetched, so the current federal regulation supplies this evidence directly.

Hiraku screener usage instructions are verified against `src/app/screening/screening-journey.tsx` and [screening evidence](../screening/SOURCES.md). They describe our own interface; official citations substantiate the screening limitation and general SNAP concepts rather than imply government endorsement of Hiraku.

Coverage is NYC SNAP basics, applying, using the existing screener, basic income/household terminology, and reviewed October work-rule changes, exemptions, alternatives, and next steps, explained in the application's selected language. Shosai may connect explicitly reviewed rules to voluntarily shared circumstances and draw conditional conclusions about individual rules, but must not predict overall eligibility or benefits. Personal eligibility questions receive supported explanations before a handoff to the limited NYC screener and HRA application process. Other-location or uncovered location-specific rules must be identified as unverified and referred to that area's official agency. Immigration, student eligibility exceptions, sanctions beyond the reviewed ABAWD time limit, individual work-rule decisions, benefits amounts, and unclear circumstances need official help. Month-only October questions receive the latest dated update and its distinction from the earlier expansion before a clarification or handoff. Pasted notices may be translated only from explicit text, with notice statements distinguished from reviewed policy; do not infer unstated reasons, deadlines, or remedies, solicit uploads, or repeat sensitive identifiers. Unrelated questions redirect to supported topics. The model has no search tools. When a user opts into screening follow-up, the model receives server-validated user-reported answers and a reproduced result for explanation only; corrections and new comparisons go through Review my answers, not conversational recalculation. Typed and reviewed-recording questions start fresh; live conversations supply bounded session history as untrusted context to understand follow-up questions. Earlier replies cannot override the reviewed context. Both paths use the same full-answer and source-ID checks before speech.

These controls are prompt-based and answers cite reviewed source excerpts with visible review dates. Shosai mentions HRA's official role for personal determinations or when confusion about its authority could affect the user's next step, rather than repeating the caveat in every answer. Server validation checks format, length, and known source IDs; it does not verify factual grounding or citation relevance. Recheck official sources before demos or when guidance may have changed; the model does not fetch live policy updates. HRA alone makes official NYC eligibility determinations.

## Personalized prompt check

On October 11, 2026, five fictional questions were sent through the configured provider and `getGuidance` using the revised prompt: a household rule, personal eligibility, an uncovered work requirement, a pasted discontinuation notice, and a household follow-up with history. All five passed the existing output validation. Manual review found supported household personalization and follow-up continuity, and no overall eligibility prediction in the income answer. The behavioral checks were not fully satisfied: the work-rule answer did not explicitly identify missing policy coverage, and the notice answer inferred that the benefit period was ending without support from the notice or reviewed context. The approved prompt was implemented unchanged; these findings remain limitations, not evidence of fully verified factual restraint. No new policy sources were added.

On October 11, 2026, the exact demo question, "I heard the SNAP work requirements changed in October. Does that mean I have to get a job to keep my benefits?", reproduced the missing-coverage refusal through the configured provider and `getGuidance`. After adding the reviewed work-rule sources and dated explanation instruction, the same question passed output validation and returned both NYC ABAWD citations. The response explained the October 1, 2026 reset, that work rules remain and restoration is not automatic, alternatives to a paid job, and possible exemptions. Automated live assertions checked the dated reset, alternatives, successful validation, and an ABAWD citation. Three deterministic source-coverage tests check the reset/enforcement distinction, supported work options and exemptions, and separate October 2025 evidence. These protect the supplied facts, not every possible model answer. Additional live personal-scenario probes were not run; microphone playback and multilingual answers were not rechecked for this change.
