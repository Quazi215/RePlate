# Shosai's reviewed guidance context

Reviewed October 10, 2026 for slice #19. `sources.server.ts` contains short paraphrases of the evidence below, not just a list of URLs. Requests do not retrieve websites. Recheck these pages before the demo and update both context and review dates if facts change.

- [NYC SNAP guide](https://www.nyc.gov/main/services/snap-benefits): SNAP basics; application, interview, and requested-document steps; HRA determination; alternative application methods; screening caveat and right to apply. Numeric benefit statements, expedited timelines, recertification specifics, and work-rule rules are deliberately excluded.
- [New York State application guide](https://www.ny.gov/services/apply-snap): gross income before taxes/deductions; different income guidelines; official application and budget requirement. No numeric income limits or benefit amounts enter the guidance context.
- [7 CFR 273.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-C/part-273/subpart-A/section-273.1): general household definition and required combinations, including spouses, children under 22 living with parents, and children under another member's parental control. The eCFR view was current through October 8, 2026 when reviewed. Exceptions exist; Shosai refers unclear individual arrangements to HRA. USDA's public eligibility page could not be fetched, so the current federal regulation supplies this evidence directly.

Hiraku screener usage instructions are verified against `src/app/screening/screening-journey.tsx` and [screening evidence](../screening/SOURCES.md). They describe our own interface; official citations substantiate the screening limitation and general SNAP concepts rather than imply government endorsement of Hiraku.

Coverage is NYC SNAP basics, applying, using the existing screener, and basic income/household terminology, explained in the application's selected language. Shosai may connect explicitly reviewed rules to voluntarily shared circumstances and draw conditional conclusions about individual rules, but must not predict overall eligibility or benefits. Personal eligibility questions receive supported explanations before a handoff to the limited NYC screener and HRA application process. Other-location or uncovered location-specific rules must be identified as unverified and referred to that area's official agency. Immigration, student exceptions, sanctions, work-rule decisions, benefits amounts, and unclear circumstances need official help. Pasted notices may be translated only from explicit text, with notice statements distinguished from reviewed policy; do not infer unstated reasons, deadlines, or remedies, solicit uploads, or repeat sensitive identifiers. Unrelated questions redirect to supported topics. The model has no search tools. When a user opts into screening follow-up, the model receives server-validated user-reported answers and a reproduced result for explanation only; corrections and new comparisons go through Review my answers, not conversational recalculation. Typed and reviewed-recording questions start fresh; live conversations supply bounded session history as untrusted context to understand follow-up questions. Earlier replies cannot override the reviewed context. Both paths use the same full-answer and source-ID checks before speech.

These controls are prompt-based and answers cite reviewed source excerpts with visible review dates. Shosai mentions HRA's official role for personal determinations or when confusion about its authority could affect the user's next step, rather than repeating the caveat in every answer. Server validation checks format, length, and known source IDs; it does not verify factual grounding or citation relevance. Recheck official sources before demos or when guidance may have changed; the model does not fetch live policy updates. HRA alone makes official NYC eligibility determinations.
